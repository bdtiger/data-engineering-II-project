\section{Results}

We evaluated three regression models on 2{,}000 GitHub repositories (80/20 train-test split) with log-transformed targets and 11 engineered features.

\subsection{Model Performance Comparison}

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Model} & \textbf{$R^2$} & \textbf{RMSLE} & \textbf{MAE (stars)} \\
    \midrule
    Linear Regression  & 0.4011 & 1.3864 & 32{,}300 \\
    SVR (RBF, C=15)    & 0.8290 & 0.7408 & 13{,}826 \\
    Neural Network     & \textbf{0.8489} & \textbf{0.6964} & \textbf{10{,}449} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Test set performance: Neural Network achieves highest $R^2$ (0.8489) and lowest MAE (10{,}449 stars).}
  \label{tab:model-results}
\end{table}

The Neural Network achieved superior performance with $R^2=0.8489$ and RMSLE=0.6964, significantly outperforming Linear Regression ($R^2=0.4011$) and SVR ($R^2=0.8290$). The 108\% improvement in $R^2$ over Linear Regression demonstrates that deep learning hierarchical feature abstraction effectively captures non-linear repository relationships (e.g., synergy between fork count, subscriber base, and activity indicators). Training required 90 epochs in 25.43 seconds with final train loss 0.4493 and validation loss 0.3081, indicating early stopping (patience=10) prevented overfitting and achieved excellent generalization.

\subsection{Horizontal Scalability Analysis}

Horizontal scalability testing evaluated system throughput across multiple worker VMs using 8 concurrent prediction tasks. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:scalability} and Table~\ref{tab:scaling}, the system demonstrates near-linear scaling efficiency across distributed workers~\cite{amdahl1967, kepner2018}.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Worker VMs} & \textbf{Time (s)} & \textbf{Speedup} & \textbf{Efficiency} \\
    \midrule
    1 & 182.0 & 1.00\texttimes & 100\% \\
    2 & 95.0 & 1.91\texttimes & 95.5\% \\
    3 & 70.0 & 2.60\texttimes & 86.7\% \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Horizontal scalability: 8 concurrent prediction tasks on 1--3 worker VMs. Near-linear speedup (1.91\texttimes at 2 VMs) demonstrates effective load distribution via RabbitMQ message broker~\cite{merkel2014}.}
  \label{tab:scaling}
\end{table}

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/Graph.jpg}
  \caption{Scalability visualization: Left plot shows wall-clock execution time decreasing from 182s (1 VM) to 70s (3 VMs). Right plot compares measured speedup (1.91\texttimes, 2.60\texttimes) against ideal linear scaling (2.0\texttimes, 3.0\texttimes). The 5--14\% deviation from ideal reflects RabbitMQ broker overhead and inter-VM communication latency.}
  \label{fig:scalability}
\end{figure}

Key observations: (1) 2-VM configuration achieves 1.91\texttimes speedup, approaching the theoretical 2.0\texttimes ideal; (2) 3-VM configuration maintains 86.7\% efficiency despite increased broker communication; (3) Scaling remains effective up to 3 VMs on ssc.medium resources (2 vCPU, 4GB RAM). The slight efficiency loss at 3 VMs is expected due to message serialization, queue contention, and network latency between worker nodes. Celery's work-stealing queue distribution ensures balanced task allocation across workers~\cite{kepner2018}. Horizontal scaling via Docker Compose worker replicas (\texttt{docker compose --scale worker\_1=N}) enables linear throughput increases without modifying application code, validating the architecture's scalability properties for production workloads.
