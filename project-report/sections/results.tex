\section{Results}

We evaluated three models on 2{,}000 repositories (80/20 split) using log-transformed targets and 11 features.

\subsection{Model Performance Comparison}

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Model} & \textbf{$R^2$} & \textbf{RMSLE} & \textbf{MAE (stars)} \\
    \midrule
    Linear Regression  & 0.4011 & 1.3864 & 32{,}300 \\
    SVR (RBF, C=15)    & 0.8290 & 0.7408 & 13{,}826 \\
    Neural Network     & \textbf{0.8489} & \textbf{0.6964} & \textbf{10{,}449} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Test set performance: Neural Network achieves highest $R^2$ (0.8489) and lowest MAE (10{,}449 stars).}
  \label{tab:model-results}
\end{table}

The Neural Network performed best with $R^2=0.8489$, outperforming Linear Regression ($R^2=0.4011$) and SVR ($R^2=0.8290$). The large gap over the linear baseline shows that non-linear interactions between features (e.g., forks and age) are important for predicting popularity. Training took 90 epochs in 25.43 seconds; early stopping (patience=10) prevented overfitting, with a final validation loss of 0.3081.

\subsection{Horizontal Scalability Analysis}

We tested throughput with 8 concurrent prediction tasks across 1, 2, and 3 worker VMs. Results are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:scalability} and Table~\ref{tab:scaling}.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Worker VMs} & \textbf{Time (s)} & \textbf{Speedup} & \textbf{Efficiency} \\
    \midrule
    1 & 182.0 & 1.00\texttimes & 100\% \\
    2 & 95.0 & 1.91\texttimes & 95.5\% \\
    3 & 70.0 & 2.60\texttimes & 86.7\% \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Horizontal scalability: 8 concurrent prediction tasks on 1--3 worker VMs. Near-linear speedup (1.91\texttimes at 2 VMs) demonstrates effective load distribution via RabbitMQ message broker~\cite{hariri2018}.}
  \label{tab:scaling}
\end{table}

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/Graph.jpg}
  \caption{Scalability visualization: Left plot shows wall-clock execution time decreasing from 182s (1 VM) to 70s (3 VMs). Right plot compares measured speedup (1.91\texttimes, 2.60\texttimes) against ideal linear scaling (2.0\texttimes, 3.0\texttimes). The 5--14\% deviation from ideal reflects RabbitMQ broker overhead and inter-VM communication latency.}
  \label{fig:scalability}
\end{figure}

At 2 VMs we achieved 1.91\texttimes speedup (95.5\% efficiency), and at 3 VMs 2.60\texttimes (86.7\% efficiency). The small drop from ideal scaling is due to RabbitMQ broker overhead and inter-VM communication latency~\cite{verbraeken2020survey}. Celery's work-stealing queue distributes tasks evenly, and adding replicas via \texttt{docker compose up -d --scale worker\_1=N} requires no code changes, making the system easy to scale in production~\cite{hariri2018}.
